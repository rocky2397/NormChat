## The headline failure: national annex front matter is a retrieval attractor

The ÖNORM B document appears in the sources for **every** question, so a
naive check ("was the national annex cited?") passes. It is the wrong part
of the document.

| Question | B clause that answers it | What was actually retrieved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q3 | `B 1992-1-1` §9.2 Querkraft, p. 21 | p. 4, p. 1, p. 4 |
| Q4 | `B 1992-1-1` §9.5 Stabwerkmodelle, p. 28 | p. 4, p. 1 |
| Q5 | `B 1992-1-1` §6.1.4, p. 12 | p. 4 |

Page 4 is the *Vorwort*; page 1 is the cover. Front matter wins because it
is dense with exactly the query vocabulary — "Nationale Festlegungen zu
ÖNORM EN 1992-1-1", "national festzulegende Parameter" — and the table of
contents repeats every clause number in the document. The operative clauses
lose because they have almost no vocabulary of their own: `§6.1.4` reads, in
full, *"Festlegung zu ÖNORM EN 1992-1-1:2015, Abschnitt 3.1.6(1)P — Der
empfohlene Wert wird übernommen."*

This is worse than the annex being missed outright. A source card reading
`ÖNORM B 1992-1-1, S. 4` looks like the national annex was consulted.

The same attractor pulls in unrelated national annexes: Q4 cites
`B 1993-1-5`, `B 1993-1-8`, `B 1993-1-9` and `B 1995-1-2` — steel and timber
documents in a reinforced-concrete question — all via their front matter.

## Q5 in detail — a confidently wrong answer

Answer given: *"Ja, die Betondruckfestigkeit muss … abgemindert werden … Der
empfohlene Wert für α_cc ist 0,85 (siehe ÖNORM EN 1992-2, Abschnitt 3.1.6)."*

Verified against the documents:

- `ÖNORM EN 1992-1-1` (Hochbau) p. 37: *"Der jeweilige landesspezifische
  Wert α_cc sollte zwischen 0,8 und 1,0 liegen. … **Der empfohlene Wert ist
  1,0.**"*
- `ÖNORM EN 1992-2` (**Brücken**) p. 16: *"Der empfohlene Wert für α_cc ist
  **0,85**."*
- `ÖNORM B 1992-1-1` §6.1.4 (the Austrian decision): *"Der empfohlene Wert
  wird übernommen."* — **never retrieved.**

So the answer sourced its number from the *bridge* part and applied it to a
general question, never surfaced the buildings recommendation of 1,0, and
never reached the Austrian national decision that settles it. Whether the
final "Ja" is defensible is an engineering call, but the reasoning path is
demonstrably wrong.

## Second bug: citation labels leak in an unhandled form

Q1's answer contains `(siehe Quelle 1 und 2)` and `(siehe Quelle 2)`.

`_replace_citation_labels` in `services/llm.py` matches `\[Quelle (\d+)\]` —
square brackets, single number. The model emitted parentheses and a compound
"1 und 2", so the deterministic backstop passed it straight through to the
user. `tests/test_llm_citation_labels.py` only covers the bracketed form.

## What this run does and does not justify

It **rules out** the theory that the national annex is simply unreachable —
it is reachable, just at the wrong pages.

It **points at** front matter (cover, Vorwort, table of contents) as
indexing noise. Excluding it at ingestion is cheap, is a chunking change
rather than a retrieval change, and directly targets the measured cause.

It **strengthens** the case for two-pass clause retrieval (take clause
numbers from the first pass, look them up lexically) — `§6.1.4` contains the
literal string `3.1.6(1)P`, so it is reachable that way once the front
matter stops crowding the results.
